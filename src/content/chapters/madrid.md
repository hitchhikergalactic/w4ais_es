    about:
      - "AI safety is still a small field whose decisions will shape where this technology goes, and it is short of people in governance, policy, operations and leadership, while many women with the right experience never get in because they have no network, no one to ask and no clear route."
      - "People in the field talk about p(doom), the estimated probability that AI ends up causing serious harm. Lowering it does not depend only on the people who train the models, but also on those who decide how AI is regulated, audited, bought and deployed, which is exactly where the field is most short of people."
      - "Whether you work in AI, cybersecurity, compliance or technical team management, or come from another field and the topic interests you, you will leave knowing what this work looks like from the inside, with contacts among the people already doing it and a clearer idea of the role you could play in it. No prior knowledge is needed."
      - "It is not a panel: after a short presentation of real AI safety problems, the conversation belongs to the room, with space to ask, disagree and bring in what each person knows from their sector. The session is in Spanish."
      - "Open to anyone interested, with a special focus on senior profiles who want to bring their experience to this field."
      - "Organised by Women for AI Safety, the professional network that helps women get into AI safety, governance and policy and grow within them, sponsored by BlueDot Impact and in collaboration with Codemotion and Celonis, who host us at their offices."
      - "Hosted by Osmani Redondo, who leads Women4AISafety and founded iaS – Inteligencia Artificial Segura, the first Spanish-language community dedicated to AI safety, and coordinates BlueDot Impact's meetups in Spain from there. Osmani came to the field from dentistry, cultural management, software development and cloud architecture, writes the weekly newsletter Semana iaS, and at this session presents the problems that open the debate and moderates the conversation."
    recap: "It started with one person, an idea and a room lent by Celonis. On 24 November the chairs filled up: talks on how to get into AI safety, real problems put on the table, and an open conversation that turned into new contacts."

The Madrid chapter brings W4AIS offline: in-person meetups, in Spanish, for women who work in AI, cybersecurity, compliance or technical team management, and for anyone coming from another field who wants to understand what working in AI safety looks like from the inside.
